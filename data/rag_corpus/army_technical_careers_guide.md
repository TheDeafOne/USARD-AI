# Technical Careers Exploration Guide

This fictional workshop document supplies approved high-level themes for discussing Army technical career families. It is not a list of current vacancies and does not establish individual eligibility, assignment, training dates, or contractual terms.

## Mechanical maintenance

Mechanical maintenance work can involve inspecting systems, identifying abnormal wear, using technical references, replacing or adjusting components, documenting completed work, and confirming that a system operates as intended. Useful classroom examples include bearings, seals, fasteners, filters, belt drives, fluid-system concepts, and the effect of contamination or poor alignment.

Presenters should emphasize that maintenance is more than replacing parts. A technician observes a symptom, gathers evidence, selects a safe test, isolates likely causes, completes authorized work, and verifies the result. Accurate documentation and tool accountability are part of the technical task.

## Diagnostics

Diagnostics combines observation, measurement, technical references, and disciplined reasoning. Broad examples may include distinguishing a mechanical problem from an electrical problem, comparing expected and observed behavior, tracing a fault through a system, and checking whether a repair corrected the original symptom.

Career events should show how technicians avoid guessing. A useful demonstration gives students two or three plausible causes and asks which inspection would provide the most useful evidence. The presenter can then connect that decision process to reliability, safety, and efficient use of parts and time.

## Electrical and electronic fundamentals

Many technical systems combine mechanical components with wiring, sensors, power distribution, and control devices. Approved talking points include reading simplified diagrams, checking connections, understanding the purpose of a sensor, and recognizing that an apparent software problem may have a physical cause. School demonstrations should use low-risk training components and should not energize unapproved circuits.

## Logistics and supply support

Technical teams rely on parts identification, inventory accountability, storage, transportation, maintenance schedules, and clear status reporting. Students may explore how a parts list supports a repair, why configuration matters, and how teams adapt when a needed item is unavailable. Do not discuss sensitive inventories, locations, readiness levels, or operational vulnerabilities.

## Quality, safety, and teamwork

Technical work includes following procedures, protecting people and equipment, communicating status, and asking for help when conditions fall outside the task. Quality checks may include measurements, visual inspection, documentation review, or an operational test. Team members often bring different specialties to the same problem.

## Structured technical training

Career exploration may describe technical training in broad terms: classroom instruction, supervised practice, evaluation, continuing development, and experience working as part of a team. Presenters should not promise a particular course, credential, timeline, assignment, or outcome. Current availability and individual qualifications must be confirmed through official counseling channels.

## Connections to civilian learning

Broadly transferable themes include troubleshooting, preventive maintenance, technical documentation, safety, logistics, communication, leadership, and disciplined problem solving. Transferability depends on the individual, the specific experience, external licensing rules, and the requirements of a future employer or educational institution.
