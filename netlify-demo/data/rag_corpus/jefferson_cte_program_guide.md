# Jefferson High School Career and Technical Education Program Guide

This fictional guide describes the themes, facilities, and learning goals of Jefferson High School's career and technical education programs. It is intended to help educators and approved partners connect career events to current classroom learning without turning a school activity into an individual eligibility or placement determination.

## Engineering design pathway

Students in the engineering design pathway practice measurement, computer-aided design, prototyping, documentation, and iterative problem solving. Early projects emphasize reading a requirement, sketching alternatives, selecting materials, and explaining why a design changed after testing. Advanced students use basic CAD models and technical drawings to communicate dimensions and tolerances.

The pathway values demonstrations that make the relationship between a drawing and a physical component visible. Appropriate examples include comparing a worn part with a serviceable part, tracing how a load moves through an assembly, explaining why tolerances matter, and showing how a technician records an inspection result.

## Robotics and automation

The robotics sequence introduces sensors, actuators, simple control logic, mechanical linkages, and troubleshooting. Students learn that a system failure can come from mechanical alignment, wiring, power, software configuration, or a faulty sensor. Instructors emphasize diagnostic discipline: observe the symptom, isolate likely causes, test safely, document the result, and verify the repair.

Career speakers should avoid presenting robotics as only programming. The department wants students to see the combined roles of mechanical maintenance, electrical fundamentals, logistics, calibration, documentation, and teamwork. Offline demonstrations are preferred because outside presenters cannot use the school network.

## Transportation and mechanical systems

The transportation module uses training assemblies and cutaway components rather than operational vehicles. Students examine fasteners, bearings, seals, gears, belt drives, filters, and fluid-system concepts. Classroom activities emphasize preventive maintenance, inspection intervals, contamination control, tool accountability, and the difference between replacing a part and diagnosing the underlying cause.

The strongest external demonstrations use visible training parts and a structured diagnostic story. Students respond well when a presenter begins with a symptom, asks the class what evidence would distinguish competing causes, and then reveals the inspection result. Presenters should connect technical work to safety, reliability, mission readiness, and responsible use of resources.

## Logistics and maintenance records

Jefferson treats logistics as part of technical work. Students practice reading parts lists, checking quantities, labeling components, planning the sequence of a task, and documenting what was used. Instructors want students to understand that maintenance depends on accurate records, available parts, safe storage, and clear communication between operators, technicians, and supervisors.

Suitable career-event topics include inventory accountability, maintenance scheduling, quality checks, technical manuals, work orders, and how a team responds when the correct part is not immediately available. Examples should remain at a broad educational level and should not include sensitive operational details.

## Workplace behaviors

Across all pathways, the department evaluates preparation, communication, teamwork, safety, and reflection. Students are expected to describe what they observed, explain why they selected a troubleshooting step, and identify what evidence would change their conclusion. Presenters should make room for questions and should distinguish personal experience from an official requirement.

## Recommended event design

The department prefers short, hands-on demonstrations supported by printed diagrams or role cards. A useful sequence is: introduce the system, show a symptom, let students identify possible causes, demonstrate a safe inspection, and close with a reflection on the careers involved. Activities should include an observation option and should not require collection of student information.

## Topics outside the program scope

The program guide does not establish current job availability, individual qualifications, compensation, incentives, contract terms, or education-benefit eligibility. Those questions must be answered through current official channels. The guide supports career exploration and alignment with classroom content only.
